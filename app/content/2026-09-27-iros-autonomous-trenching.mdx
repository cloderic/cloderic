This paper was presented as a poster at the
[5th Workshop on Future of Construction](https://construction-robots.github.io/index2026.html)
at IROS 2026, in Pittsburgh. All authors are part of my team, the
[Vortex Studio](https://docs.vortexsim.com/latest_ga/) team at
[CM Labs](https://cm-labs.com).

We look at excavator trenching, a representative earthmoving task with a long
horizon: how much soil the bucket takes when digging sets the load for every
stage that follows. We split the trenching cycle into six goal-conditioned
stages and train in Vortex Studio, simulating multibody dynamics, hydraulic
actuation and deformable terrain. At each step, the action blends the command of
an untuned resolved-rate inverse kinematics controller with the output of a Soft
Actor-Critic policy. The policy's share of authority is either fixed for a whole
run or annealed from the controller to the policy during training, an approach
we call Action Blending Curriculum over Residual Reinforcement Learning
(ABC-RRL).

Neither extreme is reliable on its own: the controller alone never completes a
cycle, and unguided reinforcement learning converged on only one seed out of
three. With classical guidance, every seed converges, and the curriculum keeps
that reliability once the policy has full authority, reaching 94.7–97.3%
success.

More details are available on the
[project page](https://cm-labs-simulations.github.io/autonomous-trenching/), and
the paper and its reviews are on
[OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=cFvZUn4lrZ).
